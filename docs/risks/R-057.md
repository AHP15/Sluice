## R-057: Forwarders on non-loopback addresses are not recognised, so discovery still routes around them

- **Severity**: medium
- **Likelihood**: medium
- **Disposition**: mitigate
- **Date**: 2026-11-07
- **Date resolved**:
- **Description**: D-040 detects the tunnel case only when a seed is loopback. Port-forwards are routinely bound elsewhere: `ssh -L 0.0.0.0:...` or a LAN address on a jump host, `socat` or HAProxy on a dev VM, a Tailscale or WireGuard address, Docker's `host.docker.internal`. With such a seed plus a seed list or SRV, discovery replaces it with the members' advertised hostnames exactly as in R-044, and no refusal fires — the guard covers the most common spelling of a tunnel, not tunnels.
- **Disposition rationale**: Medium severity at medium likelihood; no address test can recognise every forwarder, so the guard is to make the routing change visible and confirmed rather than inferred.
- **Action**: Guard: whenever discovery yields members outside the configured seeds — loopback or not — the first connection shows the actual member hosts and requires a one-time confirmation that is remembered per profile and member set, and re-asked if the set changes; the connection details always list the connected members (with D-039's DNS provenance). Tests cover the confirmation being re-requested when membership changes. Mitigating tasks: T-108.
- **Related decisions**: D-040, D-008
