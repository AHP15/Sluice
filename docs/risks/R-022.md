## R-022: catch_unwind survives the panic but leaves poisoned locks and leaked connections

- **Severity**: medium
- **Likelihood**: medium
- **Disposition**: mitigate
- **Date**: 2026-07-31
- **Description**: Wrapping IPC command boundaries in `catch_unwind` stops a driver panic from killing the process, but it does not restore invariants. A panic while a `Mutex` is held poisons it, so every later operation touching that state fails; a panic mid-stream abandons a checked-out pooled connection and an open server-side cursor or transaction. The app appears to survive while being progressively unusable until restart, which is arguably worse than crashing because the failure is not attributed to its cause.
- **Disposition rationale**: Medium severity at medium likelihood. D-003 knowingly accepted that an in-process driver panic can reach the window; what it did not account for is that surviving the panic without restoring invariants is worse than crashing, because the resulting damage is never attributed to its cause.
- **Action**: Guard: `catch_unwind` stays, but as a reporting boundary, not a recovery claim. Use non-poisoning locks (`parking_lot`) or explicitly re-initialize state on a poisoned lock rather than propagating poison indefinitely. Pooled connections return through RAII guards that run during unwinding, and any connection or pinned session live at the moment of a caught panic is marked tainted and torn down rather than reused. A caught panic surfaces as D-017's `driver bug` kind naming the affected session and writes the crash file per D-020; where core state cannot be shown intact, the app tells the user to restart rather than continuing in a progressively unusable window. Mitigating tasks: T-006.
- **Related decisions**: D-003, D-015
