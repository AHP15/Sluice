## R-029: A Mongo stub written by the contract's author confirms the contract instead of testing it

- **Severity**: medium
- **Likelihood**: high
- **Disposition**: mitigate
- **Date**: 2026-07-31
- **Description**: With the shared conformance suite dropped, the early Mongo capability descriptor and stub driver became the only guard on the cross-paradigm contract before two relational engines depend on it. But a stub written by whoever designed the contract will be shaped to the contract by construction — the author's model of Mongo is the thing under test, and it cannot detect its own gaps. The exercise produces confidence without producing validation, which is the failure mode that makes the deferred-Mongo ordering risky in the first place.
- **Disposition rationale**: Medium severity at high likelihood, and the flaw is structural — a stub written from the same mental model as the contract cannot detect that model's gaps — so the mitigation has to replace the source of evidence rather than strengthen the stub.
- **Action**: Guard: replace the paper stub with a thin but *real* Mongo driver run against an actual mongod (Docker) before SQL Server work calcifies the contract — enough to execute the navigator tree, a structured browse with filter and sort, one edit, and one raw command end to end against a real collection with heterogeneous documents. It need not be complete or shipped; it needs to be the engine, not the author's model of the engine, reporting where the contract does not fit. Record every point of friction as a contract finding, and treat the exercise as unfinished until it has produced at least one. Mitigating tasks: T-073.
- **Related decisions**: D-022, D-021, D-004
