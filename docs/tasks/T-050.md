## T-050: Per-execution row and byte budget shared across the outcome sequence

- **Status**: done
- **Size**: M
- **Implements**: D-010
- **Mitigates**: R-007, R-043
- **Depends on**: T-049, T-010
- **Acceptance**: The result cap is a per-execution budget of rows and cumulative serialized bytes consumed in order across all outcomes in the sequence, with truncation surfaced on the affected outcome and the remaining budget shown.

- **Notes**: Before this, raw scripts had **no** cap at all and
  `simple_query` materialized every row of every statement in memory. Now the
  message stream is consumed incrementally (`simple_query_raw`) through a
  `BudgetMeter`: one per-execution `ExecBudget` of rows *and* estimated
  serialized bytes (`row_cost`), whichever binds first, consumed in order
  across all outcomes; rows past it are dropped on arrival (not decoded once
  spent). The affected `Rows` outcome and every later one are marked
  `truncated`; `ExecutionResult.budget` reports limits, usage, which limit
  tripped and at which outcome, and the core's one-line summary states it
  ("display limit of 10000 rows reached at result 2 — … export to file").
  The budget limits what is shown, **never what executes**: statements after
  the exhausted one still run (verified — an INSERT after three capped SELECTs
  landed). Defaults: D-010's 10,000 rows plus a 32 MiB byte cap (value chosen
  here — confirm); `PgDriver::with_budget` overrides. Structured browse is
  bounded by its page `limit` and the per-value ceiling, not this budget.
